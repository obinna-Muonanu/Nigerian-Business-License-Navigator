## What these results mean

### What was tested

The agent was asked **31 questions** about Nigerian business licensing, the kind a real business
owner would ask: "How much does CAC registration cost?", "What goes on a NAFDAC label?", "How fast must
I report a data breach?". Three of them were asked in Pidgin (rag-29, rag-30, rag-31). Two of them
(rag-27 and rag-28) were deliberately **outside** what the knowledge base covers, to see whether the
agent would admit it doesn't know instead of making something up.

Each answer was graded by a separate, stronger model (Claude Sonnet), which compared it with the
correct section of the source documents. It gave two scores, each out of 2:

- **Correct:** 2 means the answer is right and complete, 1 means it's partly right or missing
  something important, and 0 means it's wrong or doesn't answer the question.
- **Citation:** 2 means the answer clearly says which document and section it came from, 1 means the
  source is only partly right, and 0 means there's no proper source.

### The headline numbers, in plain words

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Correctness 1.81 out of 2** | About 90% of the way to perfect. 26 of the 31 answers were fully correct, 4 were partly correct, and 1 missed the point. |
| **Citation 1.90 out of 2** | About 95%. 27 of the 29 answerable questions were cited properly. The two out-of-scope questions aren't counted, because a correct "I don't know" has nothing to cite. |
| **No-answer handled 2/2** | Both out-of-scope questions (export licences to Ghana, and rules in Kano State) were correctly declined. The agent said it didn't have that information and pointed to the right authority, instead of inventing an answer. |

**In short:** for most questions the agent gives the right answer, and it shows where it got it. When
it doesn't know, it says so.

### Where it did well

- **Fees, timelines and documents** were answered accurately across every regulator: CAC, NAFDAC,
  PCN, NDPC, NRS, SON and the trademark registry. That's the information business owners need most,
  and the part that's easiest to get wrong.
- **It flagged uncertainty when the documents did.** In rag-26, the sources say the stamp duty amount
  isn't published, and the agent said exactly that and told the user to confirm on the CAC portal.
- **Pidgin works.** Two of the three Pidgin questions (rag-29 and rag-30) got full marks.

### Where it fell short, and why

Five answers lost points. They fall into three patterns:

1. **Turning "unknown" into a definite answer** (rag-13, rag-20). The documents say certain facts
   are not stated: whether a cosmetics reseller needs their own NAFDAC permit, and whether a Tax ID
   has a fee. Instead of saying "this isn't stated, please check", the agent gave a confident "no".
   This is the most important weakness here, because the brief says the agent must never invent a
   policy or a price.
2. **Incomplete answers** (rag-07, rag-23). For the NAFDAC facility inspection question, the agent
   covered the facility rules but left out the application steps, fees and timelines. For the SON
   question, the judge marked some facts (the 60-day timeline and the three-year validity) as not
   supported. These may come from a nearby section of the documents, since the judge only sees the
   expected section, but they weren't checked separately.
3. **Asking instead of answering** (rag-31, a Pidgin question about Oyo State premises registration).
   The agent asked for the user's business type before answering. But the required documents are the
   same for every business type, so it should have listed them straight away. This was the only
   answer that scored 0.

### The bottom line

The knowledge base and the retrieval work well. The agent finds the right document and section
almost every time, and its citations are reliable. The remaining errors aren't about finding
information. They're about **how the agent behaves when the information is missing** (it sometimes
fills the gap instead of saying "unknown") and **asking a question when it could have just answered**.
Both are prompt and behaviour issues, so they could be fixed without changing the knowledge base.
