# Data protection requirements (NDPC)

## Summary
The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the NDPC's General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025 set rules for any business that collects and uses people's personal data, such as customers' names, phone numbers, addresses and payment details. (NDPC-3) The Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) enforces them.

These requirements matter most for businesses that sell online:
- **Packaged fruit drinks** that sell online
- **Online cosmetics store**
- **Furniture business** that sells online

A **retail pharmacy** may also be covered even though it sells only in person. Health is one of the sectors NDPC lists for "major importance", and pharmacies keep patient medication records. (NDPC-4) **Confirm with NDPC whether a community pharmacy must register.**

There are two layers:
1. **Obligations for every business that processes personal data**: a privacy notice, a lawful basis and consent, cookie rules, security, breach reporting and staff training. (NDPC-3)
2. **Extra obligations for "data controllers and processors of major importance" (DCPMI)**: registration with NDPC, a Data Protection Officer and annual compliance audit returns. (NDPC-3, NDPC-4) A small business becomes one of these once it handles data of **more than 200 people in six months**. (NDPC-4)

## Requirement: Check whether the business is "of major importance"
- **Requirement ID:** N01
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** packaged fruit drinks (online), online cosmetics store, furniture business (online), retail pharmacy (to confirm)
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** CAC business registration (R01)
- **Must be done before:** NDPC registration (N02)
- **Why it comes first:** only businesses of major importance must register, appoint a Data Protection Officer and file annual compliance audit returns. (NDPC-3, NDPC-4)

### Who is of major importance (NDPC-4)
A business is of major importance if it keeps or has access to a filing system (paper or digital) for personal data and either:
- processes the personal data of **more than 200 people within six months**, or
- operates in a listed sector, including finance, communication, health, education, insurance, export and import, aviation, tourism, oil and gas, and electric power.

A business based outside Nigeria that targets people in Nigeria counts as operating in Nigeria. (NDPC-3, Article 8(2))

### The three categories and registration fees (NDPC-4, February 2024)
| Category | Who it covers | Registration fee |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary High Level (OHL) | Processes data of more than 200 people in six months. Includes SMEs with access to personal data, schools, primary health centres, and agents or vendors serving larger data controllers | ₦10,000 |
| Extra High Level (EHL) | Processes data of more than 1,000 people in six months. Includes government agencies, microfinance and mortgage banks, higher institutions, and hospitals giving secondary or tertiary care | ₦100,000 |
| Ultra High Level (UHL) | Processes data of more than 5,000 people in six months. Includes commercial banks, telecoms, insurers, multinationals, oil and gas companies and payment gateways | ₦250,000 |

Each category can also be reached by meeting a set number of other factors listed in the notice. A small online store that serves more than 200 customers in six months would normally fall in the **Ordinary High Level** category. (NDPC-4)

The GAID 2025 keeps the same three categories but moves the thresholds into its Schedule 7. (NDPC-3, Article 8(4)) Schedule 7 could not be read. **Confirm the current thresholds and fees with NDPC.**

## Requirement: Register with NDPC as a data controller or processor of major importance
- **Requirement ID:** N02
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** any of the four business types that is of major importance (N01)
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** major-importance check (N01)
- **Must be done before:** annual compliance audit returns (N08)

### Details
- **Fee:** ₦10,000 for the Ordinary High Level category. (NDPC-4) See the table under N01 for the other categories.
- **Renewal:** Ordinary High Level registration must be renewed every year. Extra High and Ultra High Level businesses register once. (NDPC-3, Article 9(2)–(3))
- **Changes:** any significant change must be reported to NDPC within 60 days. (NDPC-3, Article 9(4))
- **Deadline:** businesses that already existed had to register between 30 January and 30 June 2024. (NDPC-4) The registration deadline for a new business is not stated in the sources read. **Unknown — ask NDPC.**
- **Not registering:** registering late, or not at all, is a default under the Act and attracts the penalty set out in the Act. (NDPC-4) The amount is not stated in the sources read.

### Where to apply
- The sources read do not name the registration portal. Enquiries: registration@ndpc.gov.ng; information: www.ndpc.gov.ng (NDPC-4)

## Requirement: Appoint a Data Protection Officer
- **Requirement ID:** N03
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** any of the four business types that is of major importance (N01)
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** major-importance check (N01)
- **Must be done before:** data protection impact assessment (N06), because a certified Data Protection Officer must vet it
- **What is required (NDPC-3, Articles 7(i), 11 and 12):**
  - Businesses of major importance must designate a Data Protection Officer (DPO).
  - The DPO can be an employee or engaged under a service contract.
  - The DPO's contact details must be published and sent to NDPC.

## Requirement: Publish a privacy policy, privacy notice and cookie notice
- **Requirement ID:** N04
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** packaged fruit drinks (online), online cosmetics store, furniture business (online); any business that collects personal data
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** CAC business registration (R01), because the notice must name the business
- **Must be done before:** collecting customers' personal data, for example before the online store goes live

### Privacy policy and notice (NDPC-3, Articles 7(j)–(m) and 27)
- Write and publish a privacy policy. Show privacy and cookie notices on the website's homepage.
- The privacy notice must include:
  - the business's name and the lawful basis for processing
  - what personal data is collected, why, and how
  - who else has access to the data, and why
  - customers' rights over their data
  - a contact within the business for complaints
  - the customer's right to complain to NDPC
- Giving customers information is not the same as getting their consent. (Article 27(4))

### Cookie banner (NDPC-3, Articles 7(l) and 19)
- Cookies that are not strictly necessary need the visitor's consent. It must be freely given, informed and specific.
- Strictly necessary cookies that do not process sensitive, financial or privately stored data do not need an opt-in box. All other cookies need an explicit accept or reject choice.
- The cookie banner must be conspicuous without the visitor having to scroll. It must cover a significant part of the middle, left or right of the homepage. A small banner at the bottom that can be missed is treated as a transparency failure.
- The notice must say that cookies are used and why, name the responsible business, and explain how to withdraw consent.

## Requirement: Lawful basis and consent for using customer data
- **Requirement ID:** N05
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** any of the four business types that collects personal data
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** CAC business registration (R01)
- **Must be done before:** collecting and using customers' personal data

### Lawful bases (NDPC-3, Article 16(2))
Personal data may only be processed on one of six lawful bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public interest or legitimate interest. Relying on legitimate interest needs a written Legitimate Interest Assessment. (Article 26(2)(a))

### When consent is required (NDPC-3, Article 18(1))
- Direct marketing, such as promotional emails, SMS or WhatsApp messages
- Sensitive personal data
- Using data for a new purpose that does not fit the original one
- Children's data
- Sending data to a country without an NDPC adequacy decision
- Fully automated decisions with significant effects on the person

### Rules for consent (NDPC-3, Article 17)
- Keep records of consent.
- Make withdrawing consent as easy as giving it.
- Give a clear accept or decline option for every consent request.

### Customers' rights (NDPC-3, Articles 7(s)–(u) and 36 to 38)
Make it easy for customers to request their data, correct it, move it to another provider, and have it deleted. Deletion applies, for example, when consent is withdrawn or a customer objects to direct marketing. If a sale does not go ahead, delete the data collected for it within six months unless there is a justified reason to keep it. (Article 21(2)) The deadline for responding to customers' requests is not stated in the parts of the GAID read. **Unknown.**

## Requirement: Data protection impact assessment for e-commerce
- **Requirement ID:** N06
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** packaged fruit drinks (online), online cosmetics store, furniture business (online)
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** Data Protection Officer appointed (N03), where the business is of major importance
- **Must be done before:** starting the online processing, that is, before the online store goes live
- **Why it is required:** e-commerce services, profiling and cross-border data transfers are listed among the activities that need a data protection impact assessment (DPIA). (NDPC-3, Article 28(3)(a), (j) and (o))
- **What is required (NDPC-3, Article 28):**
  - Complete the DPIA before processing begins.
  - Have it vetted by a certified Data Protection Officer.
  - File it with NDPC.
- **If it is not done:** NDPC may restrict the platforms used to collect the data. (Article 28(6))

## Requirement: Data security and breach reporting
- **Requirement ID:** N07
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** any of the four business types that collects personal data
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** CAC business registration (R01)
- **What is required (NDPC-3):**
  - Keep a security monitoring schedule covering encryption reviews, authentication checks and software updates, certified by a qualified information security officer. (Article 29)
  - Report any personal data breach to NDPC **within 72 hours** of becoming aware of it. (Articles 7(p) and 33(1))
  - Tell affected customers **immediately** if the breach poses a high risk to them, such as fraud, identity theft or exposure of sensitive data. (Articles 7(q) and 33(2)–(3))
  - Sign a data processing agreement with any outside company that processes data on the business's behalf, such as a payment, delivery or website hosting provider. The agreement must cover where the data is processed, the security measures used and how disputes are resolved. The business stays accountable for these providers. (Article 34)
  - Keep an internal data protection policy and a basic privacy checklist. (Articles 7(n) and 30(7))
  - Train staff within six months of starting business, then at least once a year. (Article 7(v))

## Requirement: Compliance audit and annual compliance audit returns
- **Requirement ID:** N08
- **Issued by:** Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Applies to:** compliance audit: any business that processes personal data; compliance audit returns: businesses of major importance (N01)
- **Applies in:** all states
- **Must be done after:** NDPC registration (N02)
- **How often:** every year

### Details (NDPC-3, Articles 7(b)–(c) and 10)
- Carry out a data protection compliance audit within 15 months of starting business, then every year.
- Businesses of major importance file a compliance audit return (CAR) every year, through NDPC's online platform:
  - Established before 12 June 2023: by 31 March each year.
  - Established after 12 June 2023: within 15 months of being established, then every year.
- Late filing adds a penalty of 50% of the filing fee. (Article 10(9))
- Ultra High and Extra High Level businesses must file through an NDPC-licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO). (Article 10(14)) Filing fees are set in the GAID's Schedule 10, which could not be read. **Unknown — confirm with NDPC.**

### Data Protection Compliance Organisations (NDPC-1)
- A DPCO is an organisation licensed by NDPC to train, audit, consult and provide data protection compliance services (Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, section 33).
- NDPC's DPCO page states that every filing by a data controller must include a "DPCO Verification Statement". The GAID requires a DPCO only for Ultra High and Extra High Level businesses. (NDPC-3) **Confirm with NDPC whether an Ordinary High Level business needs a DPCO for its filing.**

## Enforcement
- Refusing an NDPC directive to stop a privacy breach makes the business accountable as if it had committed the breach. (NDPC-3, Article 32(4))
- NDPC can issue temporary orders. (NDPC-3, Article 39(13))
- The full penalties are set out in Part X of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, which is not covered here.
